## Sports & Games
A golf ball has about 300 to 500 dimples. They help it fly roughly twice as far as a smooth ball would.
The first golf balls were made of wood. Later ones, called featheries, were leather pouches stuffed with feathers.
Astronaut Alan Shepard hit two golf balls on the Moon in 1971.
Basketball was invented in 1891 by James Naismith, a Canadian teacher, using two peach baskets as hoops.
In the earliest basketball games, the baskets still had bottoms, so the ball had to be fetched out after every score.
Volleyball was invented in 1895 by William Morgan as a gentler indoor game. He first called it Mintonette.
An Olympic gold medal is mostly silver, covered with at least 6 grams of real gold.
When the Olympic rings were designed in 1913, their colors plus the white background included a color from every competing nation's flag.
Winners at the ancient Olympic Games received a wreath of olive leaves, not a medal.
The marathon distance of 42.195 kilometers was set at the 1908 London Olympics, so the race could start at Windsor Castle.
Tennis grew out of a French game called jeu de paume, the game of the palm, which was first played with bare hands.
One theory says love, meaning zero in tennis, comes from the French word for egg, because a zero looks like an egg.
The longest tennis match in history lasted 11 hours and 5 minutes, spread over three days at Wimbledon in 2010.
Wimbledon goes through more than 50,000 tennis balls every year.
Ice hockey pucks are frozen before games so they bounce less on the ice.
The World Cup trophy was stolen in England in 1966. A week later, a dog named Pickles found it under a hedge.
The 2026 FIFA World Cup, hosted by the United States, Canada and Mexico, was the first with 48 teams.
There are more possible games of chess than there are atoms in the observable universe.
Checkmate comes from the Persian phrase shah mat, meaning the king is helpless.
The queen was once one of the weakest chess pieces. She became the most powerful piece in Europe around the year 1500.
Chess boxing is a real sport. Competitors switch between rounds of chess and rounds of boxing.
Scrabble was invented by Alfred Butts, an architect who was out of work during the Great Depression.
The king of hearts is the only king in a standard deck of cards without a mustache.
If you shuffle a deck of cards well, the order you get has almost certainly never existed before in history.
A badminton shuttlecock has been smashed at more than 500 kilometers per hour, faster than any other ball in sport.
An official baseball has 108 double stitches.
American footballs are nicknamed pigskins, but they are made of cowhide.
Every player on a Stanley Cup-winning hockey team gets to spend a day with the trophy.
The Stanley Cup has been used as a cereal bowl, and some players have even had babies baptized in it.
Sumo wrestlers throw salt into the ring before a match to purify it.
Tour de France riders cycle about 3,500 kilometers in just three weeks.
Usain Bolt reached a top speed of about 44.7 kilometers per hour during his 9.58-second 100 meter world record in 2009.
Swimmer Michael Phelps won 28 Olympic medals, 23 of them gold, more than anyone else in history.
Table tennis began in Victorian England as an after-dinner game, with a row of books used as the net.
Skateboarding and surfing became Olympic sports at the Tokyo Games, which were held in 2021.
Breaking, also known as breakdancing, made its Olympic debut at the 2024 Paris Games.
The world record for toppling dominoes is nearly 4.5 million, set in the Netherlands in 2009.
The numbers on a dartboard are arranged to punish bad aim. High numbers sit right next to low ones.
A perfect game in bowling is 300 points, which means 12 strikes in a row.
The odds of an average golfer making a hole-in-one are often put at about 12,500 to 1.
A hockey puck can fly at more than 160 kilometers per hour off a hard slapshot.
Minecraft is the best-selling video game of all time, with more than 300 million copies sold.
Tetris was created in 1984 by Alexey Pajitnov, a computer scientist in the Soviet Union.
The shape of Pac-Man was inspired by a pizza with one slice missing.
Mario was originally called Jumpman. In his first game, Donkey Kong in 1981, he was a carpenter.
Pokémon is the highest-earning media franchise of all time, ahead of Star Wars and Mickey Mouse.
Snakes and Ladders comes from an ancient Indian game that taught lessons about good and bad deeds.
The opposite sides of a standard die always add up to seven.
Senet, played in ancient Egypt about 5,000 years ago, is one of the oldest known board games.
In 1900, live pigeon shooting was an event at the Paris Olympics. It was the only time animals were killed on purpose in Olympic competition.

## Wild Planet Earth
Mount Everest is still rising by a few millimeters each year, as two tectonic plates keep pushing together.
Mount Everest is the highest mountain above sea level, but Mauna Kea in Hawaii is taller from its base on the sea floor to its top.
The summit of Mount Chimborazo in Ecuador is the point farthest from Earth's center, because Earth bulges at the equator.
The continents move about as fast as your fingernails grow.
Africa is slowly splitting in two along the East African Rift.
Antarctica holds about 70 percent of the world's fresh water, locked up as ice.
If all of Antarctica's ice melted, sea levels would rise by about 58 meters.
Russia stretches across 11 time zones.
Thanks to its overseas territories, France has more time zones than any other country.
Canada has the longest coastline of any country in the world.
The shore of the Dead Sea is the lowest land on Earth, more than 400 meters below sea level.
The Dead Sea is so salty that you float on it without even trying.
Lake Baikal in Russia holds more fresh water than all five of North America's Great Lakes combined.
Lake Baikal is the deepest lake in the world, at more than 1,600 meters deep.
The Amazon River pushes so much fresh water into the ocean that the sea stays less salty far out from the coast.
There are no bridges across the main stretch of the Amazon River.
The Nile and the Amazon compete for the title of world's longest river, depending on how they are measured.
Angel Falls in Venezuela is the tallest waterfall in the world, almost 1 kilometer high.
The Sahara is roughly the size of the United States.
Only about a quarter of the Sahara is covered in sand dunes. Most of it is rock and gravel.
The Sahara was green, with lakes, grasslands and hippos, several thousand years ago.
Parts of the Atacama Desert in Chile are so dry and Mars-like that they are used to test Mars rovers.
There are about 1,350 potentially active volcanoes on land around the world.
Volcanic eruptions can create their own lightning storms inside their ash clouds.
Yellowstone National Park sits on top of a supervolcano.
The strongest earthquake ever recorded hit Chile in 1960, with a magnitude of 9.5.
Around half a million detectable earthquakes happen every year. About 100,000 of them can be felt.
About three quarters of Earth's active volcanoes lie along the Pacific Ring of Fire.
Days on Earth are getting very slightly longer, because the Moon's tides are slowing our planet's spin.
The highest air temperature officially recorded on Earth is 56.7 degrees Celsius, in Death Valley, California, in 1913.
The lowest temperature ever measured at a weather station was minus 89.2 degrees Celsius, at Vostok Station in Antarctica in 1983.
The heaviest hailstone on record fell in Bangladesh in 1986 and weighed about 1 kilogram.
Raindrops are not tear-shaped. Small ones are round, and big ones are flattened like a hamburger bun.
Earth's magnetic north pole wanders by tens of kilometers every year.
Earth's magnetic field has flipped many times. The last full reversal was about 780,000 years ago.
The northern lights appear when particles from the Sun crash into gases high in our atmosphere.
Blood Falls in Antarctica looks like a waterfall of blood. Its color comes from iron-rich salty water that rusts in the air.
Over Lake Maracaibo in Venezuela, lightning flashes on as many as 260 nights a year.
Peru's Rainbow Mountain gets its stripes of red, yellow and turquoise from different minerals in the rock.
The Mariana Trench is deeper than Mount Everest is tall.
Greenland is the world's largest island, and about 80 percent of it is covered in ice.
Most of Earth's volcanic eruptions happen deep under the ocean, where no one sees them.
The Amazon rainforest makes much of its own rain, as trees release water vapor into the air.
Dust from the Sahara blows across the Atlantic and helps fertilize the Amazon rainforest.
Earth is the only planet in our solar system not named after a Greek or Roman god.
Earth is the densest planet in the solar system.
About 71 percent of Earth's surface is covered by water.
Australia is wider than the Moon.
Earth's air is mostly nitrogen. Oxygen makes up only about 21 percent.
Oymyakon in Russia is one of the coldest towns on Earth. Temperatures there have dropped below minus 67 degrees Celsius.

## Bugs & Creepy Crawlies
Ants have been on Earth for more than 100 million years.
Scientists estimate there are about 20 quadrillion ants on Earth, around 2.5 million for every person.
Ants have no lungs. They breathe through tiny holes in their bodies called spiracles.
Leafcutter ants do not eat the leaves they carry. They use them to grow fungus gardens, and they eat the fungus.
Some ants keep tiny insects called aphids like cattle, protecting them and collecting the sweet honeydew they make.
A honeybee may visit 50 to 100 flowers on a single trip out of the hive.
A worker honeybee makes only about one twelfth of a teaspoon of honey in her whole life.
Honeybees tell each other where to find flowers by performing a waggle dance.
Bumblebees have been seen rolling small balls around, apparently just for fun.
A queen honeybee can lay up to about 2,000 eggs in a single day.
Butterflies taste with their feet.
Monarch butterflies can migrate up to about 4,800 kilometers, from Canada to Mexico.
No single monarch butterfly makes the full round trip. It takes several generations.
Inside its chrysalis, a caterpillar breaks down into a kind of soup before rebuilding itself as a butterfly.
Mosquitoes are the deadliest animals to humans, because of the diseases they spread.
Only female mosquitoes bite. They need the blood to make eggs.
Mosquitoes track you down by sensing the carbon dioxide you breathe out.
Houseflies taste with their feet, just like butterflies.
Flies see the world in slow motion compared with us, which is why they are so hard to swat.
Dragonflies catch around 95 percent of the prey they chase, making them some of the best hunters on Earth.
Prehistoric relatives of dragonflies had wingspans of about 70 centimeters.
A cockroach can live for about a week without its head.
Cockroaches can hold their breath for about 40 minutes.
The ancestors of cockroaches were scurrying around more than 300 million years ago, long before dinosaurs.
A flea can jump more than 100 times its own body length.
You can estimate the temperature by counting how often crickets chirp. They chirp faster when it is warm.
Firefly light is almost pure light, with very little heat, making it one of the most efficient lights in nature.
Fireflies are not flies. They are beetles.
Ladybugs are beetles too, and a single ladybug can eat thousands of aphids in its life.
When threatened, ladybugs ooze a bad-tasting yellow liquid from their knees.
Beetles make up roughly a quarter of all known animal species.
Some dung beetles use the light of the Milky Way to roll their dung balls in a straight line.
One kind of dung beetle can pull more than 1,000 times its own body weight.
Spiders are not insects. Spiders have eight legs, while insects have six.
Spider silk is stronger than steel of the same weight.
Most spiders have eight eyes.
The idea that people swallow several spiders a year in their sleep is a myth.
Some spiders fly by ballooning. They release silk threads and float on the wind, sometimes for hundreds of kilometers.
Scorpions glow blue-green under ultraviolet light.
Scorpions can survive for many months without eating.
Despite their name, centipedes never have exactly 100 legs. They always have an odd number of leg pairs.
In 2021, scientists described an Australian millipede with 1,306 legs, the first known to have more than 1,000.
Termite mounds have natural air conditioning, which inspired the design of an energy-saving office building in Zimbabwe.
Some termite queens live for more than 20 years.
Earthworms have no eyes and no lungs. They breathe through their skin.
Cutting an earthworm in half does not make two worms. At best, only the head end survives.
Some stick insects grow to more than 60 centimeters long, legs included.
A praying mantis can turn its head about 180 degrees to look over its shoulder.
The bombardier beetle defends itself by spraying a boiling hot chemical from its rear end.
Some cicadas spend 17 years underground before coming out to sing, mate and die within a few weeks.

## Your Brain & Mind
Your brain contains around 86 billion nerve cells called neurons.
Your brain runs on about 20 watts of power, roughly the same as a dim light bulb.
The idea that we only use 10 percent of our brains is a myth. Brain scans show activity almost everywhere.
Your brain has no pain receptors of its own. That is why some brain surgery can be done while the patient is awake.
Your brain is about 75 percent water.
Some nerve signals travel at more than 400 kilometers per hour.
You cannot tickle yourself, because your brain predicts exactly what your own fingers will do.
Each of your eyes has a blind spot, but your brain fills in the gap so you never notice it.
Most people forget their dreams within minutes of waking up.
Most vivid dreams happen during REM sleep, when your eyes dart back and forth under your eyelids.
During REM sleep, most of your muscles are switched off, so you do not act out your dreams.
One leading theory says yawning helps cool down your brain.
Yawns are contagious. Even reading about yawning can make you yawn.
Dogs can catch yawns from humans, especially from their owners.
Your short-term memory can only hold about four to seven things at once.
People tend to remember less of information when they know they can easily look it up later.
Your brain processes social rejection in some of the same areas that handle physical pain.
The chills you get from a great song are called frisson. They are linked to a burst of the feel-good chemical dopamine.
Smells can trigger powerful memories, because your sense of smell is wired closely to the brain's memory and emotion centers.
About two out of three people have experienced déjà vu.
Your brain keeps developing until your mid-twenties. The part that plans ahead and controls impulses finishes last.
Learning changes your brain. London taxi drivers, who memorize thousands of streets, have a larger memory area called the hippocampus.
True multitasking is mostly a myth. Your brain switches rapidly between tasks and loses time with each switch.
The image on the back of your eye is upside down. Your brain flips it the right way up.
Some blind people use clicks to echolocate like bats, and their brain's vision area helps process the echoes.
Some people have synesthesia, which means they might see colors when they hear music or read numbers.
Some people cannot picture images in their mind at all. This is called aphantasia.
Some people cannot recognize faces, even of close family. This is called face blindness.
Seeing faces in clouds, electric sockets or the front of cars is called pareidolia.
You tend to like things more the more often you see them. Psychologists call this the mere exposure effect.
After you learn a new word, you suddenly seem to see it everywhere. This is called the frequency illusion.
Staying awake for about 17 to 19 hours can slow your reactions as much as having had a few alcoholic drinks.
Teenagers' body clocks naturally shift later, which makes it harder for them to fall asleep early.
You are about 30 times more likely to laugh when you are with other people than when you are alone.
Brain freeze happens when something icy cools the roof of your mouth and nearby blood vessels react quickly.
The scientific name for brain freeze is sphenopalatine ganglioneuralgia.
There are no left-brained and right-brained personality types. Both halves of your brain work together all the time.
Going for a walk can boost creative thinking, according to studies at Stanford University.
Good ideas often arrive in the shower or on a walk, after you stop thinking hard about a problem.
Many people remember the Monopoly man wearing a monocle, but he never has. Shared false memories like this are called the Mandela effect.
An adult human brain weighs about 1.3 to 1.4 kilograms.
Your memory of an event changes a little bit every time you recall it.
People remember the first and last items on a list better than the ones in the middle.
A song stuck in your head is called an earworm. One study found chewing gum can help get rid of it.
Newborn babies can already recognize their mother's voice, because they heard it in the womb.
Most adults cannot remember anything from before about age three. This is called childhood amnesia.
Your brain sees the world with a small delay, so what you see is always a fraction of a second in the past.
Humans can recognize thousands of faces. Researchers estimate the average person knows about 5,000.
Feeling a phone vibrate in your pocket when it did not is very common. It is called phantom vibration syndrome.
Your brain forms a first impression of a stranger's face in about a tenth of a second.

## Tech & the Internet
The first email was sent in 1971 by Ray Tomlinson, who chose the @ symbol to separate the user from the computer.
The World Wide Web was invented in 1989 by Tim Berners-Lee at CERN, a physics lab in Switzerland.
The very first website went online in 1991. You can still visit a copy of it today.
The internet and the World Wide Web are not the same thing. The web is just one service that runs on the internet.
The first .com domain name ever registered was symbolics.com, in 1985.
More than 5 billion people around the world use the internet.
Google's name comes from googol, the number 1 followed by 100 zeros.
One of Google's first computer storage units was built with a case made of Lego bricks.
Amazon was almost called Cadabra, as in abracadabra.
Apple's very first logo showed Isaac Newton sitting under an apple tree.
The Apple I computer went on sale in 1976 for $666.66.
The first iPhone was released in 2007.
ENIAC, one of the first general-purpose electronic computers, weighed about 27 tonnes and filled a large room.
The computer that guided Apollo 11 to the Moon had about 4 kilobytes of memory. A modern phone has millions of times more.
English Wikipedia has more than 6 million articles.
The QWERTY keyboard layout was designed in the 1870s for typewriters.
A 3.5-inch floppy disk, the object shown on many save icons, held just 1.44 megabytes.
In 1980, one of the first hard drives to hold a gigabyte weighed about 250 kilograms and cost around $40,000.
One of the very first video games, Tennis for Two, was played on a lab oscilloscope in 1958.
A Nintendo Game Boy that was badly burned in a bombing during the Gulf War still worked. It has been shown at a Nintendo store in New York.
CAPTCHA stands for Completely Automated Public Turing test to tell Computers and Humans Apart.
Wi-Fi does not officially stand for anything. It was a catchy name chosen by a branding company.
Bluetooth is named after Harald Bluetooth, a Viking king who united parts of Denmark and Norway.
The Bluetooth logo combines Harald's initials, written in Viking runes.
More than 500 hours of video are uploaded to YouTube every minute.
Spam email is named after a Monty Python comedy sketch in which the canned meat Spam is repeated endlessly.
The first spam email was sent in 1978 to about 400 people on ARPANET, an early version of the internet.
For years, the most common password in the world has been 123456.
The first tweet was sent in 2006 by Jack Dorsey. It said "just setting up my twttr."
One of the first photos ever posted on the web, in 1992, showed a comedy pop group made up of CERN employees.
The word bit is short for binary digit.
Undersea cables carry about 99 percent of the internet traffic between continents.
Sharks have been filmed biting undersea internet cables.
One of the first viruses for PC computers, called Brain, was made in 1986 by two brothers in Pakistan.
The Ctrl+Alt+Delete shortcut was created by IBM engineer David Bradley.
The first smartphone, the IBM Simon, went on sale in 1994. It had a touchscreen and could send email.
Nokia started out in 1865 as a paper mill in Finland.
Samsung began in 1938 as a small trading company selling dried fish, vegetables and noodles.
One of Sony's first products was an electric rice cooker. It often burned the rice.
ChatGPT reached an estimated 100 million users about two months after it launched, a record at the time.
Ada Lovelace is often called the first computer programmer. She wrote her program in the 1840s, for a machine never built.
The first computers were people. The job title computer was given to workers, often women, who did calculations by hand.
One of the first selfies was taken in 1839 by Robert Cornelius in Philadelphia.
The first digital camera, built at Kodak in 1975, weighed about 3.6 kilograms and needed 23 seconds to record one photo.
QR codes were invented in 1994 in Japan to keep track of car parts in factories.
The first mobile phone call was made in 1973 by Martin Cooper of Motorola, on a phone weighing over 1 kilogram.
The first message sent over ARPANET, the network that grew into the internet, was supposed to be LOGIN. The system crashed after LO.
The first online purchase is often said to be a pizza ordered from Pizza Hut's website in 1994.
Archie, created in 1990 by a student in Montreal, is often called the first internet search engine.
The Nintendo Entertainment System sold more than 60 million consoles around the world.

## Around the World
Japan is made up of more than 14,000 islands, according to a government recount in 2023.
Indonesia has more than 17,000 islands.
Canada has more lakes than any other country. By some counts, it has more than the rest of the world combined.
Finland is called the land of a thousand lakes, but it actually has about 188,000.
Finland has around 3 million saunas for about 5.6 million people.
Japan has roughly 4 million vending machines, selling everything from hot drinks to umbrellas.
Japanese trains are so punctual that rail companies hand out official delay certificates for late arrivals.
In 2017, a Japanese rail company publicly apologized because a train left 20 seconds early.
The small kingdom of Bhutan measures its success with Gross National Happiness.
Bhutan absorbs more carbon dioxide than it produces, thanks to its huge forests.
In Switzerland, it is illegal to keep just one guinea pig, because they are social animals and get lonely.
The national animal of Scotland is the unicorn.
Nepal's flag is the only national flag that is not a rectangle.
Australia has more than 10,000 beaches. Visiting a new one every day would take over 27 years.
There are more kangaroos than people in Australia.
There are about five sheep for every person in New Zealand.
Iceland has no army.
About a quarter of the Netherlands lies below sea level.
There are more bicycles than people in the Netherlands.
The Dutch are among the tallest people in the world.
Monaco is smaller than Central Park in New York City.
Vatican City is the smallest country in the world, at less than half a square kilometer.
Alaska is the westernmost and the easternmost US state, because its Aleutian Islands cross the 180th meridian.
Istanbul is a city on two continents, with parts in Europe and parts in Asia.
At the Diomede Islands, Russia and the United States are less than 4 kilometers apart.
The two Diomede Islands sit on opposite sides of the International Date Line, so their clocks are almost a day apart.
China uses a single time zone for the whole country, even though it is as wide as the continental United States.
Brazil is named after a tree, the brazilwood, which was prized for its red dye.
Argentina's name comes from the Latin word for silver.
Venezuela means little Venice.
Counting all its branches, the Great Wall of China stretches more than 21,000 kilometers.
Venice is built on more than 100 small islands and has over 400 bridges.
Bangkok's full ceremonial name is one of the longest place names in the world, with about 168 letters.
A hill in New Zealand has a Māori name that is 85 letters long.
The Welsh village of Llanfairpwllgwyngyll has a full name 58 letters long.
The town of Boring in Oregon is officially paired with the village of Dull in Scotland.
In Bulgaria, nodding your head often means no, while shaking it means yes.
In Japan, slurping your noodles is polite. It shows you are enjoying the meal.
In some parts of the world, a thumbs-up is considered a rude gesture.
Australia has the largest herd of wild camels in the world, and it exports some of them to the Middle East.
Antarctica has no single time zone. Research stations use the time of their home country or supply base.
There is a post office in Antarctica, at Port Lockroy, that sends tens of thousands of postcards every season.
India has more post offices than any other country, more than 150,000.
Three countries are completely surrounded by just one other country: Lesotho, Vatican City and San Marino.
Africa has 54 countries, more than any other continent.
Japan's bullet trains have carried billions of passengers since 1964 without a single passenger death from a crash or derailment.
The Burj Khalifa in Dubai is the tallest building in the world, at 828 meters.
Monaco, Singapore and Vatican City are the world's only three fully independent city-states.
Norway's royal guard has a penguin as an honorary officer: Sir Nils Olav, who lives at Edinburgh Zoo.
The Eiffel Tower is repainted by hand about every seven years, using around 60 tonnes of paint.

## Plants & Fungi
The largest living thing on Earth may be a honey fungus in Oregon that spreads underground across almost 10 square kilometers.
That giant honey fungus is thought to be thousands of years old.
Pando, a forest of quaking aspen trees in Utah, is actually a single organism connected by one root system.
Pando is estimated to weigh about 6,000 tonnes, making it one of the heaviest living things on Earth.
A bristlecone pine in California called Methuselah is more than 4,800 years old.
The tallest known tree is a coast redwood named Hyperion, about 116 meters tall.
The exact location of Hyperion is kept secret to protect it from visitors.
Some kinds of bamboo can grow almost 1 meter in a single day.
Bamboo is not a tree. It is a giant grass.
Young sunflowers follow the Sun across the sky during the day. Grown-up sunflowers face east.
The corpse flower smells like rotting meat to attract the flies that pollinate it.
A corpse flower can take seven years or more to bloom, and the bloom lasts only a day or two.
A Venus flytrap only snaps shut if its trigger hairs are touched twice within about 20 seconds. That way it does not waste energy on raindrops.
Venus flytraps grow wild only in a small area of North and South Carolina.
Evening primrose flowers can sense the buzz of a bee's wings and make their nectar sweeter within minutes.
Trees can share water and nutrients through underground networks of fungi, sometimes called the wood wide web.
Some trees release chemicals into the air when insects attack, and nearby trees respond by preparing their defenses.
The smell of freshly cut grass is a chemical distress signal from the injured plants.
About 90 percent of land plants team up with fungi in their roots to get water and nutrients.
Coffee and tea plants make caffeine as a natural pesticide to protect themselves from insects.
Chili plants make their spicy heat to stop mammals from eating their seeds.
Vanilla flavor comes from the seed pods of an orchid.
Orchids are one of the biggest plant families on Earth, with about 28,000 species.
Orchid seeds are as fine as dust, and one seed pod can hold millions of them.
Rafflesia has the largest single flower in the world, up to about 1 meter across.
Rafflesia has no leaves, stems or roots of its own. It lives as a parasite on a jungle vine.
The Wollemi pine was known only from fossils until a small grove was found alive in Australia in 1994.
During the bushfires of 2019 and 2020, firefighters secretly protected the last wild Wollemi pines.
Coconuts can float across oceans and sprout on faraway beaches.
The coco de mer palm grows the largest seed in the plant world, weighing up to about 25 kilograms.
Fungi are neither plants nor animals. They have a kingdom of their own.
Some mushrooms glow in the dark with a soft green light.
A fungus called Ophiocordyceps can take over the body of an ant and steer it like a zombie.
Yeast, a tiny fungus, is what makes bread rise.
Truffles are fungi that grow underground. Pigs and dogs are trained to sniff them out.
A single white truffle weighing about 1.5 kilograms once sold for $330,000.
In Holland in the 1630s, rare tulip bulbs sold for more than the price of a house.
During the Dutch famine of 1944 and 1945, many people survived by eating tulip bulbs.
Apples, pears, cherries, plums, strawberries and almonds all belong to the rose family.
Poison ivy is not a true ivy. It is related to cashews and mangoes.
Mango skin contains small amounts of urushiol, the same itchy oil found in poison ivy.
Rhubarb stalks are used in pies, but the leaves are poisonous.
Oak trees can live for 1,000 years.
In 2005, scientists grew a date palm from a 2,000-year-old seed found at the ancient fortress of Masada.
Russian scientists grew a flowering plant from 32,000-year-old seed tissue frozen in Siberian permafrost.
The Svalbard Global Seed Vault in Norway stores more than a million seed samples in case of a global disaster.
Scientists have described about 390,000 species of plants, and new ones are found every year.
An African baobab tree can store more than 100,000 liters of water in its trunk.
The sensitive plant, Mimosa pudica, folds up its leaves within seconds when you touch it.
In Borneo, tree shrews use a giant pitcher plant as a toilet, and the plant gets its nutrients from their droppings.

## Dinosaurs & Ancient Life
Birds are living dinosaurs.
Chickens are among the closest living relatives of Tyrannosaurus rex.
More time separates Stegosaurus from T. rex than separates T. rex from us.
T. rex had one of the strongest bites of any land animal ever, strong enough to crush bone.
T. rex's arms were short for its size, but each was about as long as an adult human's arm and very strong.
Many dinosaurs had feathers, including some early relatives of T. rex.
The real Velociraptor was about the size of a turkey and was covered in feathers, unlike in the movies.
The word dinosaur means terrible lizard. It was coined by scientist Richard Owen in 1842.
The first dinosaur to be scientifically named was Megalosaurus, in 1824.
Dinosaurs ruled the Earth for about 165 million years.
The non-bird dinosaurs died out about 66 million years ago, after an asteroid struck what is now Mexico.
The asteroid that wiped out the dinosaurs was about 10 kilometers wide.
The crater it left behind, called Chicxulub, is about 180 kilometers across.
Argentinosaurus may have weighed around 70 tonnes, making it one of the largest land animals ever.
Some long-necked dinosaurs had necks more than 10 meters long.
Spinosaurus was even longer than T. rex and may have spent much of its time hunting in water.
Pterosaurs, the flying reptiles, were not dinosaurs.
The giant sea reptiles of the dinosaur age, like plesiosaurs and mosasaurs, were not dinosaurs either.
Standing on the ground, the pterosaur Quetzalcoatlus was about as tall as a giraffe.
Dinosaur fossils have been found on every continent, including Antarctica.
Fossilized dinosaur poop is called coprolite, and it tells scientists what dinosaurs ate.
A T. rex skeleton named Sue is about 90 percent complete, one of the best ever found.
Sue was sold at auction in 1997 for $8.36 million.
A Stegosaurus skeleton nicknamed Apex sold for $44.6 million in 2024, a record for a fossil.
Triceratops had a beak like a parrot's.
Ankylosaurus had a heavy club of bone at the end of its tail.
Some dinosaurs swallowed stones to help grind up food in their stomachs.
Maiasaura means good mother lizard. Fossils show it cared for its young in nests.
Woolly mammoth tusks could grow more than 4 meters long.
The saber-toothed cat Smilodon had upper canine teeth up to about 28 centimeters long, roots included.
Megalodon, a giant prehistoric shark, may have grown to about 15 meters or more.
Megalodon died out about 3.6 million years ago.
Titanoboa, a snake about 13 meters long, lived around 60 million years ago in what is now Colombia.
Some giant ground sloths were as big as an elephant.
Glyptodons were giant armadillo relatives about the size of a small car.
Arthropleura, an ancient millipede, could grow about 2.6 meters long.
Around 700 million years ago, Earth may have been almost completely covered in ice. Scientists call this Snowball Earth.
Life on Earth began at least 3.5 billion years ago.
For most of Earth's history, all life was made of single cells.
Stromatolites, rocky layers built by bacteria, are among the oldest fossils, about 3.5 billion years old.
The coelacanth, a fish known only from fossils and thought to be extinct for 66 million years, was found alive in 1938.
Horseshoe crabs have been around for about 450 million years, and their blue blood is used to test medicines for safety.
Trilobites, armored sea creatures, survived for nearly 300 million years.
About 99 percent of all species that have ever lived are now extinct.
Earth has gone through five great mass extinctions.
The worst mass extinction, the Great Dying about 252 million years ago, wiped out most species in the ocean.
About 300 million years ago, oxygen levels were so high that some insects grew to enormous sizes.
In 2016, scientists found a feathered dinosaur tail preserved in a piece of amber.
Humans and non-bird dinosaurs never lived at the same time. They missed each other by more than 60 million years.
Scientists name around 50 new dinosaur species every year.

## Numbers & Records
The number zero was developed in India. Mathematician Brahmagupta wrote rules for using it around the year 628.
A googolplex is 1 followed by a googol of zeros. There is not enough room in the known universe to write it out.
Counting to one million, one number per second, would take about 11 and a half days.
Counting to one billion the same way would take about 32 years.
In a room of just 23 people, there is a better than 50 percent chance that two of them share a birthday.
111,111,111 times 111,111,111 equals 12,345,678,987,654,321.
Pi has been calculated to more than 100 trillion digits.
NASA uses only about 15 digits of pi for its most precise space navigation.
Pi Day is celebrated on March 14, which is also Albert Einstein's birthday.
A jiffy is a real unit of time. In computing and electronics, it often means one tick of a system clock.
A baker's dozen is 13.
The number 4 is considered unlucky in Japan and China, because it sounds like the word for death.
Many tall buildings skip the 13th floor in their numbering.
Fear of the number 13 is called triskaidekaphobia.
The tallest person ever recorded, Robert Wadlow, was 2.72 meters tall.
The oldest person whose age has been verified, Jeanne Calment of France, lived to 122 years and 164 days.
A cheetah can reach speeds of up to about 100 kilometers per hour in short bursts.
The peregrine falcon is the fastest animal on Earth, diving at more than 300 kilometers per hour.
The longest recorded flight by a chicken lasted 13 seconds.
In 1964, a high school student named Randy Gardner stayed awake for about 11 days for a science fair project.
Guinness World Records no longer accepts attempts at going without sleep, because they are too dangerous.
The world's population passed 8 billion people in November 2022.
Roughly 360,000 babies are born around the world every day.
The most common surnames in the world are Chinese ones, such as Wang, Li and Zhang, each shared by around 100 million people.
A day has 86,400 seconds.
You will spend about a third of your life asleep.
Your heart will beat about 3 billion times during an average lifetime.
When people are asked for their favorite number, the most popular answer is 7.
In Old English and Old Norse, the word hundred could mean 120. This was called the long hundred.
Roman numerals have no symbol for zero.
The ancient Maya counted in twenties, not tens.
We have 60 minutes in an hour thanks to the ancient Babylonians, who counted in sixties.
The equals sign was invented by Welsh mathematician Robert Recorde in 1557, because he was tired of writing "is equal to."
Light travels about 30 centimeters in one billionth of a second. Computer pioneer Grace Hopper handed out wires that length to show it.
A ream of paper has 500 sheets.
The YouTube video "Baby Shark Dance" has been watched more than 15 billion times, the most of any video.
The Simpsons is the longest-running American scripted prime-time TV series.
The Harry Potter books have sold more than 600 million copies.
In 2010, a traffic jam near Beijing stretched about 100 kilometers and lasted for more than 10 days.
Park ranger Roy Sullivan was struck by lightning seven times, and survived all seven.
After breathing pure oxygen first, free divers have held their breath underwater for more than 24 minutes.
There are 293 different ways to make change for a US dollar using coins.
Forty is the only number whose letters are in alphabetical order.
Every calendar year has at least one Friday the 13th, and some years have three.
As a boy, mathematician Carl Friedrich Gauss is said to have added up all the numbers from 1 to 100 in seconds. The answer is 5,050.
If you write out every number in words, you won't need the letter b until you reach one billion.
The number 2,520 can be divided evenly by every whole number from 1 to 10.
Mount Everest is 8,849 meters tall, as measured jointly by China and Nepal in 2020.
The longest-living animal ever found was a quahog clam named Ming, which was 507 years old.
A single strand of spider silk long enough to wrap around the Earth would weigh less than half a kilogram.

## Christmas & Holidays
The first text message ever sent, in 1992 in the United Kingdom, said "Merry Christmas."
Jingle Bells was one of the first songs played in space. In 1965, Gemini 6 astronauts played it on a harmonica and small bells.
Decorated Christmas trees became popular in Britain after Queen Victoria's German husband, Prince Albert, brought the tradition with him.
Electric Christmas tree lights were first used in 1882 by Edward Johnson, a business partner of Thomas Edison.
Tinsel was originally made from thin strips of real silver.
The famous Christmas tree at Rockefeller Center in New York started in 1931, when construction workers put up a small tree.
Every year since 1947, Norway has sent a Christmas tree to London's Trafalgar Square to thank Britain for its help in World War II.
Coca-Cola did not invent Santa's red suit. Cartoonist Thomas Nast drew him that way in the 1800s, though Coca-Cola's ads helped make the look famous.
Santa Claus is based on Saint Nicholas, a bishop who lived in what is now Turkey in the 300s.
In the Netherlands, Sinterklaas arrives by steamboat in mid-November, and children get gifts on December 5.
Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer was created in 1939 for a booklet given away by a department store.
Male reindeer usually lose their antlers by early December, but females keep theirs through winter. So Santa's reindeer are probably all female.
Reindeer eyes change color with the seasons, from gold in summer to deep blue in winter.
The name mistletoe may come from Old English words meaning dung on a twig, because birds spread its seeds in their droppings.
Mistletoe is a parasite that grows on trees and steals some of their water and nutrients.
Christmas celebrations were banned in England in the 1640s and in Boston from 1659 to 1681.
In 1914, British and German soldiers held an unofficial Christmas truce, and some even played football in no man's land.
White Christmas by Bing Crosby is the best-selling single of all time, with an estimated 50 million copies sold.
Mariah Carey's All I Want for Christmas Is You, released in 1994, returns to the top of the charts almost every December.
In China, apples are popular Christmas Eve gifts, because the word for Christmas Eve sounds like the word for apple.
In Japan, millions of families eat fried chicken from KFC at Christmas, thanks to a famous advertising campaign from 1974.
Iceland has 13 Yule Lads instead of one Santa, with names like Spoon Licker, Door Slammer and Sausage Swiper.
In Iceland, people give each other books on Christmas Eve and spend the night reading. It is called Jólabókaflóð, the Christmas book flood.
In Catalonia in Spain, children feed a smiling log called Caga Tió and then hit it with sticks until it poops out sweets.
In Norway, some families hide their brooms on Christmas Eve so that witches cannot steal them for a ride.
In Ukraine, Christmas trees are often decorated with spider web ornaments, from a folk tale about a poor widow's tree.
In Austria and nearby regions, a horned creature called Krampus is said to punish naughty children in early December.
In Italy, a kind witch called La Befana brings presents to children on the night of January 5.
In Spain, people eat 12 grapes at midnight on New Year's Eve, one for each chime of the clock, for good luck.
In Denmark, people throw old plates at their friends' doors on New Year's Eve. The bigger the pile, the more friends you have.
The first people to celebrate the New Year each year live on Kiribati's Line Islands in the Pacific Ocean.
The last places on Earth to reach the New Year are two uninhabited US islands, Baker Island and Howland Island.
The New Year's Eve ball drop in Times Square in New York started in 1907.
January 1 became the start of the year in ancient Rome. Julius Caesar's new calendar confirmed it in 46 BC.
Halloween grew out of Samhain, an ancient Celtic festival marking the end of the harvest.
The first jack-o'-lanterns in Ireland and Scotland were carved from turnips, not pumpkins.
In Japan, women give chocolate to men on Valentine's Day, and men return the favor a month later on White Day.
Americans eat more than 40 million turkeys every Thanksgiving.
Decorative wrapping paper took off in 1917, when the Hall brothers, founders of Hallmark, ran out of tissue paper and sold fancy envelope liners instead.
Gingerbread houses became popular in Germany after the Brothers Grimm published Hansel and Gretel.
Hanging stockings comes from a legend in which Saint Nicholas dropped gold coins down a chimney into stockings drying by the fire.
Writing Xmas is not disrespectful. The X stands for the Greek letter chi, the first letter of Christ in Greek.
The Twelve Days of Christmas are the 12 days from Christmas Day to Epiphany on January 6.
Buying every gift from The Twelve Days of Christmas would cost more than $46,000, according to one bank's yearly Christmas Price Index.
Charles Dickens published A Christmas Carol in 1843, and the first printing sold out by Christmas Eve.
A typical Christmas tree takes about 7 to 10 years to grow before it is cut.
The first artificial Christmas trees were made in Germany from goose feathers dyed green.
Since 1955, the US military has tracked Santa on Christmas Eve, today through NORAD. It started when a newspaper ad printed the wrong phone number and children called a military hotline.
Canada Post gave Santa his own postal code: H0H 0H0.
Rovaniemi in Finland, right on the Arctic Circle, calls itself the official hometown of Santa Claus.

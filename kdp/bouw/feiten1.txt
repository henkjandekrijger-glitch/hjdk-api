## Your Body Is Weird
An adult has 206 bones, but a newborn baby has around 300. Many of them fuse together as you grow.
About half of all your bones are in your hands and feet: 54 in your hands and 52 in your feet.
The smallest bone in your body is the stapes, deep inside your ear. It is about the size of a grain of rice.
The femur, your thigh bone, is the longest and strongest bone in your body.
The hyoid bone in your throat is the only bone in your body that does not touch any other bone.
Babies are born with kneecaps made of soft cartilage. They slowly turn into bone between the ages of about 3 and 5.
Humans and giraffes have the same number of neck bones: seven.
Your fingers have no muscles inside them. They are pulled by tendons connected to muscles in your palm and forearm.
You are about 1 centimeter taller in the morning than at night, because the discs in your spine get squeezed during the day.
Your heart beats around 100,000 times every single day.
Laid end to end, all the blood vessels in an adult body would stretch roughly 100,000 kilometers, enough to circle Earth twice.
An adult body holds about 5 liters of blood.
A red blood cell lives for about 120 days before it is recycled.
Your body contains about 3 to 4 grams of iron, roughly enough to make a small nail.
Your stomach makes hydrochloric acid strong enough to dissolve zinc.
The lining of your stomach renews itself every few days, so the acid does not digest your own stomach.
The rumbling sound your stomach makes has a scientific name: borborygmus.
Your small intestine is about 6 meters long, folded up neatly inside your belly.
Your liver is the only internal organ that can regrow. Even after losing most of its tissue, it can grow back to full size.
Your skin is your largest organ.
Your brain makes up only about 2 percent of your body weight, but uses about 20 percent of your energy.
If you uncoiled the DNA from just one of your cells, it would be about 2 meters long.
Humans and chimpanzees share about 98.8 percent of their DNA.
Fingernails grow two to three times faster than toenails.
Identical twins have identical DNA, but they do not have identical fingerprints.
Your iris has a pattern so unique that it can be used like a fingerprint to unlock devices.
The cornea, the clear front of your eye, has no blood vessels. It takes its oxygen straight from the air.
The average person blinks around 15 to 20 times every minute.
Your eyes cannot move smoothly on their own. Without something moving to follow, they jump in tiny quick steps called saccades.
Your pupils get bigger when you look at something you find exciting or interesting.
The human eye can tell apart roughly one million different colors.
Try it: you cannot hum while pinching your nose shut, because humming needs air to escape through your nose.
Tooth enamel is the hardest substance in your body, but it cannot grow back once it is gone.
Your taste buds are replaced every one to two weeks.
The famous tongue map, with sweet at the tip and bitter at the back, is a myth. Every part of the tongue can taste every flavor.
Umami, the savory fifth taste, was identified by Japanese chemist Kikunae Ikeda in 1908.
Your mouth makes between half a liter and one and a half liters of saliva every day.
Goosebumps are a leftover from ancestors with far more body hair. Raised hair made them look bigger and kept them warmer.
The average person has about 100,000 hairs on their head.
Your body glows. A 2009 study in Japan found that humans give off visible light, about 1,000 times too weak for our eyes to see.
An American man named Charles Osborne had the hiccups for about 68 years without stopping.
The largest muscle in your body is the gluteus maximus, the muscle in your bottom.
The masseter, your jaw muscle, can clench your teeth with surprising force for its small size.
Your ears seem to keep getting longer as you get older.
About 1 in 10 people are left-handed.
You lose tens of thousands of skin cells every minute.
People can live a normal life without their appendix, spleen, gallbladder or one of their kidneys.
Your heart has its own electrical system, which is why it can keep beating for a short time even outside the body.
A sneeze can spray thousands of tiny droplets into the air around you.
You produce a new layer of skin roughly every month.

## Animal Oddities
A group of flamingos is called a flamboyance.
A group of owls is called a parliament.
A group of crows is called a murder.
Flamingos are born gray or white. They turn pink from pigments in the shrimp and algae they eat.
Cows have best friends. Studies show they get stressed when separated from their favorite companion.
Koalas have fingerprints so similar to ours that they could confuse a crime scene investigator.
A koala sleeps up to 20 hours a day.
Wombats produce cube-shaped poop.
Sea otters hold hands while they sleep so they do not drift apart.
An octopus has three hearts and blue blood.
Cats spend about two thirds of their lives asleep.
A cat cannot taste sweetness.
Dogs can smell somewhere between 10,000 and 100,000 times better than humans.
A dog's nose print is unique, just like a human fingerprint.
Adult elephants cannot jump. Their legs are built for carrying huge weight, not for springing off the ground.
An elephant's trunk has tens of thousands of muscles.
Elephants can recognize themselves in a mirror, something very few animals can do.
Giraffes have black or dark blue tongues that can be about 50 centimeters long.
A giraffe only needs around 30 minutes to two hours of sleep a day.
Hippos cannot swim. They walk or bounce along the bottom of rivers instead.
Hippos produce a reddish oily liquid on their skin that works like sunscreen.
A rhino's horn is made of keratin, the same material as your fingernails.
Polar bears have black skin under their fur, and their fur is actually see-through, not white.
Penguins live almost only in the Southern Hemisphere, so polar bears and penguins never meet in the wild.
Emperor penguin dads keep their egg warm on their feet for about two months without eating.
An ostrich's eye is bigger than its brain.
Ostriches can run at about 70 kilometers per hour.
Hummingbirds are the only birds that can fly backwards.
A hummingbird's heart can beat more than 1,000 times per minute.
Owls cannot move their eyes, so they turn their heads instead, up to about 270 degrees.
Crows can recognize individual human faces and remember people who treated them badly.
Parrots can live for more than 60 years.
A woodpecker's tongue wraps around the back of its skull to cushion its brain.
Bats are the only mammals that can truly fly.
Vampire bats share blood meals with hungry roost mates.
A sloth can take up to a month to digest a single meal.
Sloths only climb down from their trees about once a week to go to the bathroom.
Kangaroos cannot easily walk backwards.
A baby kangaroo, called a joey, is about the size of a jelly bean when it is born.
The platypus is a mammal that lays eggs.
Male platypuses have venomous spurs on their back legs.
Horses cannot breathe through their mouths.
Goats have rectangular pupils, which give them a wide view of their surroundings.
Pigs can learn to play simple video games with a joystick, as a 2021 study showed.
Rats laugh when they are tickled, though the sound is too high for humans to hear.
Squirrels forget where they buried many of their nuts, which helps plant new trees.
A snail's tongue-like ribbon, the radula, is covered in thousands of tiny teeth.
Some lizards can drop their tail to escape a predator and grow a new one later.
A chameleon's tongue can be longer than its whole body.
The axolotl can regrow lost legs, parts of its heart and even parts of its brain.

## Under the Sea
More than 80 percent of the ocean has never been mapped in high detail, explored or even seen by humans.
The ocean produces about half of the oxygen we breathe, mostly thanks to tiny plankton.
The deepest known point in the ocean, the Challenger Deep, is about 11 kilometers down.
Below about 1,000 meters, the ocean is completely dark. No sunlight reaches that deep.
The blue whale is the largest animal known to have ever lived, bigger than any dinosaur.
A blue whale's heart is about the size of a bumper car and weighs around 180 kilograms.
A blue whale's call can be louder than a jet engine.
Sperm whales sleep vertically, floating upright in the water like giant logs.
Dolphins sleep with one half of their brain at a time so they can keep breathing.
Dolphins give each other names, using signature whistles to call specific individuals.
Orcas, or killer whales, are actually the largest members of the dolphin family.
A shark's skeleton is made of cartilage, not bone.
Sharks have been around longer than trees.
Some sharks can lose and replace thousands of teeth during their lifetime.
The Greenland shark can live for around 400 years, the longest known lifespan of any vertebrate.
Sharks can smell tiny traces of blood in the water from hundreds of meters away.
Seahorse fathers are the ones who get pregnant and give birth.
Seahorses swim upright and are among the slowest fish in the ocean.
Starfish have no brain and no blood.
A starfish can regrow a lost arm, and some can grow a whole new body from one arm.
Starfish eat by pushing their stomach out of their mouth and onto their food.
Jellyfish have existed for more than 500 million years.
Jellyfish are about 95 percent water.
One species of jellyfish, Turritopsis dohrnii, can turn back into a young form, earning it the nickname immortal jellyfish.
An octopus can taste with its arms.
Octopuses can change both the color and the texture of their skin in a fraction of a second.
Octopuses have been seen using coconut shells as portable shelters.
A group of jellyfish is called a smack.
Clownfish are all born male. The dominant one can change to female.
Pufferfish contain a toxin that is far more poisonous than cyanide.
Some fish, like the mudskipper, can walk on land.
Coral reefs cover less than 1 percent of the ocean floor but are home to about a quarter of all ocean species.
The Great Barrier Reef is the largest structure built by living creatures and can be seen from space.
Sea turtles can hold their breath for hours while resting.
Sea turtles find their way back to the beach where they were born using Earth's magnetic field.
The ocean's average depth is about 3.7 kilometers.
There are rivers and lakes on the ocean floor, made of extra salty water that is heavier than the water around it.
Sound travels about four times faster in water than in air.
Giant and colossal squids have eyes as big as dinner plates, the largest in the animal kingdom.
Lobsters taste with their legs.
Shrimp hearts are located in their heads.
The mantis shrimp punches so fast that the water around its claw briefly boils and flashes with light.
Mantis shrimp have far more color receptors in their eyes than humans.
The ocean contains about 97 percent of all the water on Earth.
The Pacific Ocean is wider than the Moon.
There is gold dissolved in seawater, but it is spread so thin that no one has found a cheap way to collect it.
Anglerfish lure prey in the dark with a glowing fishing rod on their head.
Most deep sea creatures can make their own light. This is called bioluminescence.
Eels travel thousands of kilometers to the Sargasso Sea to breed.
Narwhal tusks are actually long teeth that grow through the upper lip.

## Space Is Strange
One day on Venus is longer than one year on Venus.
Venus spins in the opposite direction from most planets, so the Sun rises in the west there.
Venus is the hottest planet in the solar system, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.
A year on Mercury lasts just 88 Earth days.
Jupiter is so big that all the other planets in the solar system could fit inside it.
Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a storm larger than Earth that has been raging for at least 150 years.
Saturn is so light for its size that it would float in a bathtub big enough to hold it.
Saturn's rings are made mostly of chunks of ice, some as small as dust and some as big as houses.
Uranus spins on its side, probably because something huge crashed into it long ago.
It rains diamonds deep inside Neptune and Uranus, according to many scientists.
Neptune has the fastest winds in the solar system, over 2,000 kilometers per hour.
Pluto is smaller than Earth's Moon.
Mars has the tallest volcano in the solar system, Olympus Mons, about two and a half times taller than Mount Everest.
Sunsets on Mars look blue.
Mars looks red because its surface is covered in iron oxide, which is basically rust.
Footprints left on the Moon by astronauts could last for millions of years because there is no wind to blow them away.
The Moon is moving away from Earth by about 3.8 centimeters every year.
We always see the same side of the Moon, because it spins exactly once for each trip around Earth.
The Sun makes up about 99.8 percent of all the mass in our solar system.
About 1.3 million Earths could fit inside the Sun.
Sunlight takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth.
When you look at the stars, you are looking into the past, because their light left them long ago.
A teaspoon of material from a neutron star would weigh billions of tons on Earth.
There are more stars in the universe than grains of sand on all of Earth's beaches, according to many estimates.
Space is completely silent, because there is no air to carry sound.
Astronauts can grow up to about 5 centimeters taller in space, because their spines stretch without gravity.
Astronauts on the International Space Station see around 16 sunrises and sunsets every day.
The International Space Station travels at about 28,000 kilometers per hour.
In space, two pieces of clean bare metal can stick together permanently. This is called cold welding.
Astronauts report that space smells like seared steak, hot metal or burnt gunpowder.
Halley's Comet passes by Earth about every 75 to 76 years. It will next appear in 2061.
A day on Earth used to be shorter. Hundreds of millions of years ago, a day lasted only about 22 hours.
Earth is not a perfect sphere. It bulges at the equator.
The footprint of the Milky Way is so big that light takes about 100,000 years to cross it.
Our galaxy is on a collision course with the Andromeda galaxy, but the crash is billions of years away.
A light year is a distance, not a time. It is how far light travels in one year, about 9.5 trillion kilometers.
The first animals in space were fruit flies, launched by the United States in 1947.
A dog named Laika was the first animal to orbit Earth, in 1957.
Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin walked on the Moon in 1969, while Michael Collins circled above.
Twelve people have walked on the Moon, all between 1969 and 1972.
The Voyager 1 probe, launched in 1977, is the most distant human-made object.
There is a cloud of alcohol in space, near the center of our galaxy, large enough to fill many solar systems.
Some planets orbit two suns at once, just like Tatooine in the movies.
Black holes do not suck things in like a vacuum. Objects must come very close to be trapped.
The Sun is about 4.6 billion years old and is roughly halfway through its life.
There are more trees on Earth than stars in the Milky Way, according to some estimates.
Olympus Mons on Mars is so wide that if you stood on its edge, the top would be beyond the horizon.
Shooting stars are not stars at all. They are tiny bits of space rock burning up in our atmosphere.
The Moon has moonquakes.
On the Moon, you would weigh about one sixth of what you weigh on Earth.

## Food Facts
Honey never spoils. Archaeologists have found pots of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs that were still edible.
Bananas are berries, but strawberries are not.
Peanuts are not nuts. They are legumes, related to beans and peas.
Cashews grow hanging from the bottom of a fruit called the cashew apple.
Pineapples take about two years to grow.
Apples float in water because about a quarter of their volume is air.
Carrots were mostly purple or yellow before orange carrots became popular in the Netherlands in the 1600s.
Tomatoes are fruits. In 1893, the US Supreme Court still ruled that they count as vegetables for tax purposes.
Avocados are fruits, and technically single-seeded berries.
Potatoes were the first vegetable grown in space, in 1995.
Chocolate was once used as money by the Aztecs, who traded with cacao beans.
White chocolate contains no cocoa solids, only cocoa butter, milk and sugar.
Ketchup was sold as a medicine in the United States in the 1830s.
The world's most expensive spice by weight is saffron. It comes from the threads of a crocus flower.
Vanilla is the second most expensive spice, because each flower must usually be pollinated by hand.
Most wasabi served in restaurants outside Japan is actually horseradish colored green.
Popcorn pops because the water inside each kernel turns to steam and bursts the shell.
A single spaghetti noodle is called a spaghetto.
Fortune cookies were most likely invented in the United States, not in China.
Ripe cranberries bounce. Farmers have used this to test their quality.
Coffee beans are actually the seeds of a cherry-like fruit.
Coffee is one of the most traded farm products in the world.
Cucumbers are about 95 percent water.
Lemons do contain sugar. Their strong acid simply hides the sweetness.
Peppers get their heat from a chemical called capsaicin. Birds cannot taste it, so they happily eat chili seeds.
The heat of chili peppers is measured on the Scoville scale.
Almonds are seeds of a fruit related to peaches and cherries.
Strawberries wear their seeds on the outside, about 200 per berry.
Broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, kale and Brussels sprouts were all bred from the same wild plant.
Cheese is one of the most stolen foods in the world.
The holes in Swiss cheese are made by gas bubbles from bacteria.
Ancient Romans sometimes paid workers partly in salt, and the word salary is linked to the Latin word for salt.
Pizza Margherita is said to be named after Queen Margherita of Italy, who visited Naples in 1889.
Hawaiian pizza was invented in Canada.
The sandwich is named after the Earl of Sandwich, an English nobleman.
Nutmeg can cause hallucinations if eaten in very large amounts.
Rice is the main food for more than half of the people on Earth.
Eating a lot of carrots can turn your skin slightly orange. The effect is harmless.
Raw kidney beans contain a toxin. They must be boiled properly before eating.
Peanut butter can be turned into diamonds under extreme pressure, because it contains carbon.
Most of the flavor you taste actually comes from your sense of smell.
Gelatin, used in gummy candy, is made from animal collagen.
Ice cream was once a luxury dessert enjoyed mainly by kings and nobles.
The croissant is strongly linked to Austria. French bakers made it famous later.
Pringles were once at the center of a UK court case over whether they count as potato crisps. The court said yes, so they are taxed like crisps.
Bubble gum is usually pink because the first successful batch was made with the only food coloring on hand, which was pink.
Water boils at a lower temperature on top of a high mountain, so pasta takes longer to cook there.
One cacao pod holds about 20 to 60 beans.
Mushrooms are more closely related to humans than to plants.
Watermelons are about 92 percent water.

## Money & Business
John D. Rockefeller is often called the richest American ever. At his peak, his fortune equaled about 1.5 percent of the entire US economy.
Rockefeller liked to hand out shiny dimes to people he met, including children.
Warren Buffett bought his first stock at the age of 11.
Buffett still lives in the house in Omaha he bought in 1958 for $31,500.
Jeff Bezos started Amazon in his garage in 1994, selling only books.
Apple's third co-founder, Ronald Wayne, sold his 10 percent share of the company in 1976 for $800.
Nike's famous swoosh was designed in 1971 by graphic design student Carolyn Davidson, who was paid $35.
Years later, Nike's founder gave Carolyn Davidson a gold swoosh ring and company shares as a thank-you.
Nike started out as Blue Ribbon Sports, selling Japanese running shoes out of a car trunk.
In 1999, Google's founders offered to sell their company to Excite for about $1 million. Excite said no.
In 2000, Blockbuster turned down the chance to buy Netflix for $50 million.
Harland Sanders was 62 when he began franchising Kentucky Fried Chicken.
Ray Kroc was 52 and selling milkshake machines when he started working with the McDonald brothers.
In 1914, Henry Ford doubled his workers' pay to $5 a day, so they could afford the cars they built.
The New York Stock Exchange began in 1792 with an agreement signed under a buttonwood tree.
Wall Street got its name from a wooden wall built by Dutch settlers in the 1600s.
After the crash of 1929, the Dow Jones index did not get back to its old peak until 1954.
Lego came close to bankruptcy in the early 2000s, before becoming one of the most valuable toy companies in the world.
Starbucks is named after Starbuck, the first mate in the novel Moby-Dick.
The domain name voice.com sold for $30 million in 2019.
Instagram had just 13 employees when Facebook bought it for $1 billion in 2012.
Facebook bought WhatsApp for about $19 billion in 2014.
There is no record that Albert Einstein ever called compound interest the eighth wonder of the world. The quote is almost certainly made up.
Rolex is owned by a charitable foundation set up by its founder, Hans Wilsdorf.
IKEA founder Ingvar Kamprad was famously frugal and drove the same old Volvo for decades.
The first credit card made of plastic appeared in 1959, from American Express.
Harley-Davidson started in a small wooden shed in Milwaukee in 1903.
Hewlett-Packard was founded in a Palo Alto garage in 1939 that is now a historic landmark.
The term bull market may come from how a bull strikes upward with its horns, while a bear swipes down with its paws.
Coca-Cola sold an average of just nine drinks a day in its first year.

## Food, BBQ & Meat
The word barbecue probably comes from barbacoa, a word of the Taíno people of the Caribbean for a wooden cooking frame.
A Texas-style brisket can smoke for 12 hours or more.
The pink smoke ring in barbecue comes from a reaction between smoke gases and the meat's pigment. It does not mean the meat is undercooked.
Searing a steak does not seal in the juices. It adds flavor through a browning process called the Maillard reaction.
Low and slow cooking turns the tough collagen in cuts like brisket and ribs into soft gelatin.
Japanese wagyu beef is graded by marbling, with A5 at the very top.
Real Kobe beef must come from Tajima cattle raised in Hyogo Prefecture in Japan.
The hamburger is named after the German city of Hamburg.
Americans eat about 7 billion hot dogs between Memorial Day and Labor Day.
Buffalo wings were created in Buffalo, New York, in 1964, at a restaurant called the Anchor Bar.
Americans eat over a billion chicken wings on Super Bowl weekend.
The official hottest chili pepper in the world is Pepper X, at about 2.7 million Scoville heat units.
A medium-rare steak has a center temperature of about 55 to 57 degrees Celsius.
Ribeye and tenderloin are so tender because those muscles do very little work on the cow.
A T-bone steak is two steaks in one: a strip steak and a piece of tenderloin, separated by the bone.
The word jerky comes from ch'arki, a word in Quechua, the language of the Inca.
The Big Mac was invented in 1967 by Jim Delligatti, a McDonald's franchise owner near Pittsburgh.
Tabasco sauce has been made on Avery Island, Louisiana, since 1868. The pepper mash ages in oak barrels for up to three years.
Sriracha is named after the seaside town of Si Racha in Thailand.
Worcestershire sauce was created in the 1830s by two chemists in Worcester, England, named Lea and Perrins.
The Caesar salad was invented in Tijuana, Mexico, by restaurant owner Caesar Cardini, not in ancient Rome.
Nachos were invented in 1943 by Ignacio Anaya, whose nickname was Nacho, in Piedras Negras, Mexico.
The chicken nugget was invented in the 1950s by Cornell University food scientist Robert C. Baker.
Belgium and France both claim to have invented french fries.
A well-seasoned cast iron pan can last for generations and gets better with use.
Charcoal briquettes were popularized by Henry Ford, who turned leftover wood from car production into fuel. The business became Kingsford.
The Weber kettle grill was invented in 1952 by George Stephen, who cut a metal buoy in half.
Pulled pork is usually made from pork shoulder, a cut also known as Boston butt, even though it does not come from the rear.
Pitmasters wrap brisket in butcher paper to push through the stall, a long pause when the meat stops heating up because moisture is evaporating from its surface.
Steak sauce A1 was created by a chef to King George IV of England in the 1820s.

## Beer, Coffee & Whiskey
Beer is one of the oldest drinks in the world. The Sumerians brewed it more than 5,000 years ago.
The workers who built the Egyptian pyramids received beer as part of their daily rations.
The German beer purity law of 1516 allowed beer to be made only from water, barley and hops. Yeast had not been discovered yet.
Oktoberfest began as a royal wedding celebration in Munich in 1810.
Despite its name, most of Oktoberfest takes place in September.
India Pale Ale was brewed with extra hops, which helped it survive the long sea voyage from Britain to India, according to the popular story.
Guinness looks black, but it is actually a very dark ruby red.
The Guinness Book of Records began after a brewery boss argued about which game bird was the fastest in Europe.
Scotch and Canadian whisky are usually spelled without an e, while Irish and American whiskey usually keep it.
Bourbon must be made in the United States, from at least 51 percent corn, and aged in new charred oak barrels.
Bourbon does not have to come from Kentucky, although about 95 percent of it does.
Scotch whisky must be aged in oak casks in Scotland for at least three years.
The whisky that evaporates from barrels as it ages, about 2 percent a year, is called the angels' share.
The Jack Daniel's distillery is in Moore County, Tennessee, a county where selling alcohol was banned for decades.
According to legend, coffee was discovered by an Ethiopian goat herder named Kaldi, whose goats got lively after eating coffee berries.
Finland drinks more coffee per person than any other country.
A shot of espresso has less caffeine than a regular mug of drip coffee.
Decaf coffee still contains a small amount of caffeine.
Kopi luwak, one of the most expensive coffees in the world, is made from beans that have been eaten and passed by civets.
Beethoven reportedly counted exactly 60 coffee beans for every cup he made.
Coffee was briefly banned in Mecca in 1511.
Prohibition in the United States lasted from 1920 to 1933.
During Prohibition, doctors could legally prescribe whiskey as medicine.
Under EU rules, only sparkling wine from the Champagne region of France can be called Champagne.
A bottle of Champagne holds about three times the air pressure of a car tire.
Beer comes in brown bottles because light can give it a skunky taste, and brown glass blocks most of that light.
The skunky smell in light-struck beer comes from a chemical closely related to a skunk's spray.
Belgium brews well over 1,000 different beers.
The Czech Republic drinks more beer per person than any other country.
The world's oldest operating brewery, Weihenstephan in Germany, traces its brewing back to the year 1040.

## Survival & the Great Outdoors
A popular survival rule of thumb says you can last about 3 minutes without air, 3 days without water and 3 weeks without food.
Moss does not reliably grow on the north side of trees, so it makes a poor compass.
Trying to suck venom out of a snakebite does not work, and medical experts advise against it.
Eating snow to quench your thirst cools your body down. Melting it first is much safer.
About 90 percent of people struck by lightning survive.
Lightning can strike up to about 16 kilometers away from a storm, out of a seemingly clear sky.
If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck by lightning, according to weather services.
Quicksand cannot swallow you whole. The human body floats in it, but moving slowly is the way out.
A grizzly bear can run at about 55 kilometers per hour, much faster than any human.
Running from a bear can trigger it to chase you, according to national park rangers.
Hypothermia can happen well above freezing, especially if you are wet and it is windy.
A 9-volt battery touched to steel wool can start a fire.
A clear night is usually colder than a cloudy one, because clouds act like a blanket and trap heat.
A rattlesnake adds a new segment to its rattle each time it sheds its skin.
The Appalachian Trail is about 3,500 kilometers long. Hiking it end to end usually takes five to seven months.
Yellowstone, founded in 1872, was the first national park in the world.
The Old Faithful geyser in Yellowstone erupts about every 60 to 110 minutes.
Drinking seawater makes dehydration worse, because your body needs extra water to get rid of the salt.
The largest fish ever caught on a rod and reel was a great white shark of about 1,208 kilograms, landed in Australia in 1959.
A compass points to magnetic north, not true north. The difference is called declination.
The North Star sits almost directly above the North Pole, so it always shows you which way is north.
In the Southern Hemisphere, the Southern Cross constellation can help you find south.
Paracord got its start in World War II parachutes. The common type is rated to hold about 250 kilograms.
A whistle can be heard much farther away than a shout and uses less energy, which is why it belongs in every survival kit.
In deserts, the temperature can drop more than 20 degrees Celsius after sunset.
The colder the air, the faster a dead phone battery drains. Keeping your phone close to your body helps it last longer.
Cotton clothes are a bad choice in cold, wet weather, because wet cotton loses almost all its insulation. Hikers say cotton kills.
More hikers in national parks need rescue because of tiredness, falls and darkness than because of wild animals.
Fire needs three things to burn: heat, fuel and oxygen. Firefighters call it the fire triangle.
A single match can start a fire that burns for hours, but most outdoor fires start with tiny dry tinder, not big logs.

## Wild Animals
Honey badgers have thick, loose skin and can survive bites from venomous snakes that would kill most animals their size.
The saltwater crocodile has one of the strongest bites ever measured in any animal.
A crocodile's jaw-opening muscles are surprisingly weak. A strong rubber band can hold its mouth shut.
Elephants communicate with low rumbles that can travel several kilometers through the ground.
A bald eagle's nest can weigh more than a tonne. The biggest one on record weighed about two tonnes.
The bald eagle nearly disappeared from the lower 48 US states because of the pesticide DDT, but recovered and was taken off the endangered list in 2007.
Wolves can travel up to 50 kilometers in a single day.
A wild wolf pack is usually a family, parents and their pups. The idea of the alpha wolf came from studies of unrelated wolves in captivity.
Moose can dive up to about 6 meters underwater to eat plants.
Bison can run at about 55 kilometers per hour and jump almost 2 meters high.
Bison injure more visitors to Yellowstone than bears do.
Hippos are considered one of the most dangerous large animals in Africa.
Komodo dragons can eat up to 80 percent of their body weight in a single meal.
Bull sharks can live in fresh water and have been found more than 1,000 kilometers up the Mississippi River.
An electric eel can produce a shock of up to about 860 volts.
A charging rhino can reach about 50 kilometers per hour.
A golden eagle can dive at more than 240 kilometers per hour.
The cassowary is often called the most dangerous bird in the world. Each foot has a dagger-like claw about 12 centimeters long.
A thirsty camel can drink more than 100 liters of water in about 10 minutes.
A camel's hump stores fat, not water.
Wolverines are only about the size of a medium dog, but can bring down a caribou in deep snow.
A polar bear was once tracked swimming 687 kilometers over nine days without stopping.
Lions can sleep up to 20 hours a day.
Tigers have striped skin, not just striped fur.
No two tigers have exactly the same stripes.
A tiger's roar can be heard up to about 3 kilometers away.
A gorilla mostly eats plants, and a big male can eat up to about 18 kilograms of food a day.
A grizzly bear's sense of smell is many times better than a bloodhound's.
Peregrine falcons have special bony cones in their nostrils that help them breathe during high-speed dives.
An adult male African elephant can weigh more than 6 tonnes.

## Movies & TV Behind the Scenes
The T. rex roar in Jurassic Park was made by mixing sounds from a baby elephant, a tiger and an alligator.
The hum of the lightsabers in Star Wars came from an old film projector motor mixed with TV interference.
Darth Vader's breathing was recorded by sound designer Ben Burtt breathing through a scuba regulator.
Harrison Ford was working as a carpenter when George Lucas cast him as Han Solo.
Sean Connery wore a hairpiece in every one of his James Bond films.
The mechanical shark in Jaws was nicknamed Bruce. It broke down so often that Steven Spielberg showed it less, which made the movie scarier.
Sylvester Stallone wrote Rocky himself and refused to sell the script unless he could play the lead.
Rocky was filmed in 28 days on a budget of about $1 million, and went on to win Best Picture.
Tom Cruise really climbed the outside of the Burj Khalifa, the world's tallest building, for Mission: Impossible – Ghost Protocol.
Jackie Chan has broken his nose, fingers, ankles and skull doing his own stunts.
In The Terminator, Arnold Schwarzenegger speaks only about 17 lines.
The cat Marlon Brando holds in the opening scene of The Godfather was a stray found on the studio lot.
Nakatomi Plaza in Die Hard is really Fox Plaza in Los Angeles, the headquarters of the studio that made the film.
Eric Stoltz played Marty McFly in Back to the Future for weeks before he was replaced by Michael J. Fox.
The Lord of the Rings trilogy was filmed all at once in New Zealand, with about 438 days of main filming.
Viggo Mortensen broke two toes kicking a helmet in The Two Towers, and the take was kept in the movie.
Buddy Ebsen was first cast as the Tin Man in The Wizard of Oz, but the aluminum dust in his makeup made him seriously ill.
The Shawshank Redemption flopped in cinemas in 1994, then became one of the most loved movies ever through TV and video.
Breaking Bad was meant to be set in California, but moved to New Mexico partly because of tax incentives for filming.
In the last two seasons of Friends, each of the six main actors earned $1 million per episode.
The contents of the briefcase in Pulp Fiction are never revealed.
Christopher Nolan flipped a real 18-wheeler truck on a Chicago street for The Dark Knight.
Most of the car stunts in Mad Max: Fury Road were done for real, not with computer effects.
The Aston Martin DB5 from the Bond film Goldfinger sold for about $6.4 million in 2019.
The Wilhelm scream, a stock sound effect from 1951, has been used in hundreds of films, including Star Wars and Indiana Jones.
In Raiders of the Lost Ark, Harrison Ford was ill on set, so instead of a long sword fight, Indiana Jones simply shoots the swordsman.
Top Gun was such a hit in 1986 that the US Navy set up recruiting tables near some cinemas.
The famous Rocky steps at the Philadelphia Museum of Art have 72 steps.
For Cast Away, Tom Hanks lost about 25 kilograms, and filming paused for a year so he could lose the weight.
The Godfather's author, Mario Puzo, said he wrote the book mainly to make money and had never met a real gangster.

## Rock & Roll
Elvis Presley wanted a rifle for his 11th birthday, but his mother bought him a guitar instead.
Elvis never performed a concert outside North America. His only shows abroad were a few in Canada in 1957.
Elvis's manager, Colonel Tom Parker, was not a real colonel. He was born Andreas van Kuijk in the Netherlands.
Decca Records turned down the Beatles in 1962.
The Beatles have 20 number one hits on the US Billboard Hot 100, more than any other act.
In April 1964, the Beatles held all top five places on the Billboard Hot 100 at the same time.
Ringo Starr was not the Beatles' first drummer. Pete Best played with them before him.
Jimi Hendrix was left-handed and often played a right-handed guitar turned upside down and restrung.
Before he was famous, Jimi Hendrix served in the US Army's 101st Airborne Division.
Queen's Bohemian Rhapsody has no chorus.
Brian May of Queen built his famous guitar, the Red Special, with his father, partly from wood of an old fireplace.
Brian May finished a PhD in astrophysics in 2007, decades after he started it.
Freddie Mercury was born Farrokh Bulsara on the island of Zanzibar.
AC/DC guitarist Angus Young started wearing his school uniform on stage after his sister suggested it.
Led Zeppelin got its name from a joke that the band would go down like a lead balloon.
The band dropped the a in Lead so Americans would not pronounce it leed.
Pink Floyd's The Dark Side of the Moon spent more than 900 weeks on the Billboard 200 album chart.
The Rolling Stones' tongue logo was designed in 1970 by art student John Pasche, who was paid about £50.
Johnny Cash never served a prison sentence. He spent only a few nights in jail, despite his famous prison concerts.
Johnny Cash's 1968 concert album recorded at Folsom Prison revived his career.
Metallica is the only band to have played on all seven continents in a single year, including a 2013 show in Antarctica.
The members of KISS did not appear in public without their makeup until 1983.
Eddie Van Halen made two-handed tapping on the guitar famous.
Eddie Van Halen played the guitar solo on Michael Jackson's Beat It for free.
In January 1992, Nirvana's Nevermind knocked Michael Jackson's Dangerous off the top of the US album chart.
Michael Jackson's Thriller is the best-selling album of all time.
About 400,000 people came to the Woodstock festival in 1969.
Chuck Berry's Johnny B. Goode is on the golden records aboard the Voyager spacecraft, now outside our solar system.
The Fender Stratocaster guitar was introduced in 1954.
Bob Dylan won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 2016.

## The Male Body
A man's beard grows about 1 to 1.5 centimeters a month.
Testosterone levels are usually highest in the morning.
The Adam's apple is the front of the thyroid cartilage, which grows larger in most men during puberty.
About 1 in 12 men is color-blind, compared with about 1 in 200 women.
Muscle comes back faster after a break because muscle cells keep extra nuclei from earlier training. Scientists call this muscle memory.
Your body uses about 10 percent of the energy in your food just to digest it.
Your heart pumps about 7,000 liters of blood every day.
Male pattern baldness is strongly linked to genes from both parents, not just your mother's side of the family.
Wearing a hat does not cause baldness.
Men snore more often than women on average.
Men's skin is about 25 percent thicker than women's on average.
On average, men sweat more than women.
Around the world, men live about five years less than women on average.
The goal of 10,000 steps a day started as a marketing slogan for a Japanese pedometer in 1965.
Grip strength is a surprisingly good predictor of overall health and long life, according to large studies.
Cracking your knuckles does not cause arthritis. One doctor cracked the knuckles of only one hand for 60 years and found no difference.
Men have nipples because every embryo starts out on the same body plan before sex hormones take over.
Sperm production needs a temperature a few degrees below normal body temperature.
The male body makes millions of new sperm every day.
A man's voice deepens at puberty because his vocal cords grow longer and thicker.
Muscle burns more calories at rest than fat does.
Exercise releases endorphins and other chemicals that improve mood.
The average adult needs seven to nine hours of sleep a night.
Your body has around 600 muscles, depending on how you count them.
Your skeleton is constantly rebuilt. Roughly every 10 years, most of your bone tissue has been replaced.
The human body is about 60 percent water.
Men's brains are on average slightly larger than women's, but brain size does not decide intelligence.
Your nose and ears do not keep growing for your whole life. The cartilage gets softer and stretches, which makes them look bigger.
Hair grows faster in summer than in winter.
Muscle makes up about 40 percent of an average man's body weight.

## Odd Laws, Rules & Records
Selling chewing gum has been banned in Singapore since 1992, except for medical gum.
In England and Wales, it is an offence to handle salmon in suspicious circumstances, under the Salmon Act 1986.
A British law from 1872 makes it an offence to be drunk in charge of a horse, cattle or a steam engine on a public road.
In London, flying a kite to the annoyance of others in the street has been an offence since 1839.
Under an old statute, whales and sturgeon washed ashore in Britain belong to the monarch as royal fish.
The British monarch also owns all unmarked mute swans on open water.
In France, a person can marry someone who has died, with special permission from the president.
In Finland, speeding fines are based on income. In 2023, a millionaire was fined about €121,000 for driving too fast.
Many stretches of Germany's autobahn have no general speed limit, but running out of fuel there can get you fined.
Voting is compulsory in Australia, and not voting can earn you a fine.
Canada has a law that limits how many coins of each kind you can use to pay for something.
In Japan, a 2008 law requires waistline measurements for adults aged 40 to 74 during health checkups.
In Venice, feeding pigeons in St Mark's Square can get you fined.
In Rome, sitting on the Spanish Steps has been banned since 2019.
The Antarctic Treaty bans military activity and mining in Antarctica.
In golf, you may carry no more than 14 clubs in your bag.
Since 2025, a soccer goalkeeper who holds the ball for more than eight seconds gives away a corner kick.
At top chess tournaments, a player can lose the game if their phone rings.
A tennis ball must bounce between 135 and 147 centimeters high when dropped from 254 centimeters.
An official NHL hockey puck weighs between 156 and 170 grams.
In the official rules of Monopoly, landing on Free Parking wins you nothing. The money pile is a house rule.
Under Monopoly's official rules, the bank never goes broke. If it runs out, the banker can write money on ordinary paper.
The perfect game in darts, 501 points with just nine darts, is called a nine-darter.
A basketball hoop is 10 feet high because that was the height of the balcony rail where James Naismith hung the first peach baskets.
The longest professional baseball game lasted 33 innings, played in 1981 between Pawtucket and Rochester.
The longest Major League game by innings went 26 innings in 1920 and ended in a tie.
The longest NFL game lasted 82 minutes and 40 seconds of play, a playoff between Kansas City and Miami in 1971.
The longest boxing match on record lasted 110 rounds, more than seven hours, in 1893. It was declared no contest.
The NBA's 24-second shot clock was introduced in 1954 to stop teams from stalling.
Ram Singh Chauhan of India had a moustache measured at 4.29 meters in 2010, the longest on record.

## Gadgets & Tools
The Phillips screw became popular after General Motors used it on its 1936 Cadillacs.
In Canada, the square-headed Robertson screw is standard. P.L. Robertson invented it in 1908.
The hook on the end of a tape measure is loose on purpose. It slides by its own thickness so inside and outside measurements are both correct.
The small black diamonds on many tape measures, every 19.2 inches, mark spacing for floor and roof trusses.
The red numbers every 16 inches on a tape measure mark the standard spacing of wall studs.
WD-40 stands for water displacement, 40th formula.
WD-40 was first used to protect the outer skin of a missile from rust and corrosion.
Tim Leatherman invented his multitool after a road trip where a simple pocketknife was not enough to fix his car.
The earliest chainsaws, in the late 1700s, were medical tools, not tools for cutting wood.
Flashlights got their name because early batteries could only power short flashes of light.
The lawnmower was invented in 1830 by Edwin Budding, inspired by a machine that trimmed cloth.
Black & Decker made the first portable electric drill with a pistol grip and trigger switch in 1917.
Black & Decker helped develop cordless tools for NASA's Apollo missions, to drill and collect Moon samples.
GPS was built by the US military. It was opened to everyone after a Korean airliner strayed off course and was shot down in 1983.
Edwin Land invented the Polaroid instant camera after his 3-year-old daughter asked why she couldn't see a photo right away.
The first home video game console, the Magnavox Odyssey, went on sale in 1972.
One of the first TV remotes, in 1950, was called Lazy Bones and was connected to the TV by a cable.
The nickname clicker comes from the 1956 Zenith Space Command remote, which made a click as small metal rods struck inside it.
The Sony Walkman came about in 1979 partly because Sony's co-founder wanted to listen to music on long flights.
A light bulb in a fire station in Livermore, California, has been burning almost continuously since 1901.
Jacob Schick sold the first electric razor in 1931.
The vacuum flask was invented by scientist James Dewar in 1892 to keep chemicals cold, not to keep coffee hot.
The first electric toaster that popped up the bread on its own was patented in 1921.
The ballpoint pen was patented by László Bíró in 1938. In many countries, a ballpoint is still called a biro.
The adjustable spanner was invented in 1891 by Swedish inventor Johan Petter Johansson.
Bubble levels work with a bubble in a vial of alcohol, which does not freeze in cold weather.
The first pocket calculators of the early 1970s cost hundreds of dollars.
The first commercially successful microwave oven for homes, in 1967, cost about $495.
The first cell phone, demonstrated in 1973, offered about 30 minutes of talk time after 10 hours of charging.
Duct tape was nicknamed duck tape because the original cotton backing repelled water like a duck's feathers.

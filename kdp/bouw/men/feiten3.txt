## Cars & Engines
The first car to go faster than 100 kilometers per hour was an electric car called La Jamais Contente, in 1899.
Ferdinand Porsche built one of the first hybrid cars, the Lohner-Porsche, around 1900.
The original Mini of 1959 mounted its engine sideways, so about 80 percent of the car's floor space was left for people and luggage.
The Ford Mustang went on sale in 1964 and took about 22,000 orders on its very first day.
The body of the first Chevrolet Corvette, in 1953, was made of fiberglass.
Diesel engines have no spark plugs. They squeeze air until it is hot enough to ignite the fuel.
Rudolf Diesel, inventor of the diesel engine, vanished from a ship crossing the English Channel in 1913.
France was the first country to require license plates on cars, in 1893.
The first car with factory air conditioning was the 1940 Packard.
The 24 Hours of Le Mans has been run since 1923. The winning cars now cover more than 5,000 kilometers in a single day.

## Planes & Pilots
A Boeing 747 burns about 4 liters of fuel every second while cruising.
In a test in 2010, the wings of a Boeing 787 were bent upward by about 7.6 meters without breaking.
Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International is the busiest airport in the world by number of passengers.
Air Force One is the radio call sign for any Air Force plane carrying the US president, not the name of one specific plane.
The first scheduled passenger airline flight, in 1914, carried one passenger across Tampa Bay in 23 minutes.
Pilots use the phonetic alphabet, Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, so that letters are never confused over the radio.
The Douglas DC-3 first flew in 1935, and some are still flying today.
The B-52 bomber first flew in 1952 and is planned to stay in service into the 2050s.
Aircraft tires are filled with nitrogen, which does not feed a fire and keeps its pressure better than air when temperatures change.
In 1986, the Rutan Voyager flew all the way around the world without stopping or refueling. The flight took nine days.

## Ships & the Sea
The Panama Canal saves a ship sailing from New York to San Francisco about 13,000 kilometers.
In 1928, adventurer Richard Halliburton swam the length of the Panama Canal and paid a toll of 36 cents, based on his weight.
The bulb that sticks out under the waterline at the front of many big ships reduces drag and saves fuel.
Sailors traditionally got a swallow tattoo for every 5,000 nautical miles they had sailed.
The story that posh means port out, starboard home is a myth. There is no evidence for it.
HMS Victory, Admiral Nelson's flagship at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805, is still a commissioned ship of the Royal Navy.
The sinking of the Lusitania in 1915 helped turn American opinion against Germany in World War I.
Modern container shipping was invented by an American trucking boss, Malcom McLean, in 1956.
Davy Jones' locker is an old sailors' expression for the bottom of the sea.
Today's largest cruise ships are about 365 meters long and carry more than 7,000 passengers.

## Military History
Samurai swords were folded and hammered many times to spread the carbon evenly and remove impurities from the steel.
On D-Day, American paratroopers used small metal clickers called crickets to identify each other in the dark.
In 1943, British intelligence floated a dead body carrying fake invasion plans off the coast of Spain to fool Germany. It was called Operation Mincemeat.
In the Great Escape of 1944, 76 men crawled out of the prison camp Stalag Luft III through a tunnel nicknamed Harry.
Only three of the 76 men in the Great Escape made it all the way to freedom.
Britain's Victoria Cross medals are cast from bronze taken from old cannons, kept just for that purpose.
American soldiers in World War II spread the doodle Kilroy was here everywhere they went.
The siege of Leningrad in World War II lasted 872 days.
King Harold of England died at the Battle of Hastings in 1066, traditionally said to be from an arrow in the eye.
Military camouflage was developed in World War I with the help of painters, who disguised guns, ships and lookout posts.

## Explorers & Adventurers
Shackleton's men survived on Elephant Island for about four and a half months, living under two upturned lifeboats.
Christopher Columbus died still believing he had reached Asia.
America is named after Amerigo Vespucci, an Italian explorer who realized the lands were a new continent.
Roald Amundsen was the first person undisputed to reach both the South Pole and the North Pole, the second by airship in 1926.
In 1909, Ernest Shackleton got within about 180 kilometers of the South Pole and turned back to save his men's lives.
Sacagawea carried her baby son with her across the American West on the Lewis and Clark expedition.
Mount Everest is named after George Everest, a British surveyor who never saw the mountain and pronounced his name Eve-rest.
Edmund Hillary also reached the South Pole overland in 1958, driving farm tractors.
In 2002, Steve Fossett became the first person to fly alone around the world in a balloon without stopping.
The Silk Road was not one road but a network of trade routes stretching more than 6,000 kilometers.

## Toughest Men in History
In 1848, railroad worker Phineas Gage survived an iron rod blasting straight through his skull.
In 1996, climber Beck Weathers was left for dead high on Everest, woke up and walked back into camp on his own.
In 1912, Irish sailor Tom Crean walked about 56 kilometers alone across Antarctic ice to save a dying comrade.
In 2013, ship's cook Harrison Okene survived for almost three days in an air pocket inside a sunken tugboat at the bottom of the sea.
In 1980, Terry Fox ran almost a full marathon every day for 143 days on one leg, across Canada.
Dick Hoyt and his son Rick, who could not walk, finished more than 1,000 races together, including Ironman triathlons.
During the 1996 Everest disaster, guide Anatoli Boukreev went back out alone into the storm and brought three climbers to safety.
In 1994, runner Mauro Prosperi got lost in a sandstorm during a race across the Sahara and survived for nine days.
In 1954, writer Ernest Hemingway survived two plane crashes in two days.
On his crawl back from the crevasse, Joe Simpson set himself goals of 20 minutes at a time with his watch, and kept meeting them.

## Sports Legends
Babe Ruth's record of 714 home runs lasted until Hank Aaron passed it in 1974.
Hank Aaron received piles of hate mail during his chase of the record, kept going anyway and finished with 755 home runs.
Jim Thorpe won Olympic gold in both the pentathlon and the decathlon in 1912.
Bobby Orr is the only defenseman in NHL history to lead the league in scoring, and he did it twice.
Bill Russell won 11 NBA championships in 13 seasons with the Boston Celtics.
In 1973, the racehorse Secretariat won the Belmont Stakes by 31 lengths, a record that still stands.
Swimmer Mark Spitz won seven gold medals at the 1972 Olympics.
Emmitt Smith is the NFL's all-time leading rusher, with more than 18,000 yards.
Jerry Rice holds the NFL record for career receiving yards, more than 22,000.
Cyclist Eddy Merckx won the Tour de France five times and was nicknamed The Cannibal because he wanted to win everything.

## Space & Astronauts
John Glenn, the first American to orbit the Earth, went back to space in 1998 at the age of 77.
Apollo 12 was struck by lightning twice during launch in 1969, and still made it to the Moon.
Astronauts train for spacewalks in a giant pool at NASA that holds a full-size copy of parts of the space station.
NASA's training plane, nicknamed the Vomit Comet, gives about 25 seconds of weightlessness on each dive.
In sunlight, the surface of the Moon gets hotter than 120 degrees Celsius. At night it drops below minus 170.
The American flags planted on the Moon have most likely been bleached white by decades of harsh sunlight.
In 2001, Pizza Hut paid to have a pizza delivered to the International Space Station.
Canadian astronaut Chris Hadfield recorded a music video while living on the space station in 2013.
SpaceX's Starship is the largest and most powerful rocket ever flown.
A spacewalk usually lasts six to eight hours, and astronauts wear adult diapers for it.

## Engineering Marvels
The Brooklyn Bridge, opened in 1883, was the first suspension bridge built with steel wire cables.
When the Brooklyn Bridge's chief engineer fell ill, his wife Emily Roebling took over much of the day-to-day work of finishing it.
The two halves of the Channel Tunnel, dug from England and France, met in 1990 less than 40 centimeters out of line.
Each of the four presidents' faces on Mount Rushmore is about 18 meters tall.
Australians nicknamed the Sydney Harbour Bridge the Coathanger.
Kansai International Airport in Japan was built on an artificial island in Osaka Bay.
Venice stands on millions of wooden piles driven deep into the mud of its lagoon.
Seattle's Space Needle was built for the 1962 World's Fair in about 400 days.
Big Ben is the name of the great bell, not the clock tower in London.
The Hollywood sign originally read Hollywoodland. It was put up in 1923 to advertise a housing development.

## Inventors & Big Ideas
The very first US patent was granted in 1790 to Samuel Hopkins, for a better way of making potash, an ingredient in fertilizer.
Alfred Nobel made his fortune from dynamite and left most of it to fund the Nobel Prizes.
Louis Braille invented his reading system for blind people when he was just 15.
Lego was founded in 1932 by Danish carpenter Ole Kirk Christiansen.
NASA engineer Lonnie Johnson invented the Super Soaker water gun.
The Goodyear tire company was named after inventor Charles Goodyear decades after his death. He had nothing to do with it.
Earle Dickson invented the Band-Aid in 1920 for his wife, who often cut herself in the kitchen.
Arthur Wynne published the first crossword puzzle in a newspaper in 1913.
The common Gem paper clip design was never patented.
In 1876, Alexander Graham Bell's first words on the telephone were a request for his assistant, Mr. Watson, to come to him.

## Money & Business
The term Ponzi scheme comes from Charles Ponzi, whose investment fraud collapsed in 1920.
Walmart has been the world's largest company by revenue for most years since the early 2000s.
In 2018, Apple became the first US company to be worth $1 trillion on the stock market.
Microsoft's first product was a version of the programming language BASIC for an early home computer, the Altair 8800.
Toyota began as a company making looms for weaving cloth.
The secret formula for Coca-Cola is kept in a vault at the World of Coca-Cola museum in Atlanta.
The old Twitter bird logo was named Larry, after basketball star Larry Bird.
The arrow in Amazon's logo points from A to Z, a hint that it sells everything.
FedEx founder Fred Smith said he once kept his company alive by winning $27,000 at blackjack in Las Vegas.
Bill Gates and Paul Allen founded Microsoft in 1975, and Gates dropped out of Harvard to run it.

## Food, BBQ & Meat
Brining meat in salt water changes its proteins so they hold on to more moisture during cooking.
Mesquite gives smoked meat the strongest flavor of the common smoking woods. Fruit woods like apple and cherry are the mildest.
Kansas City, Memphis, Texas and the Carolinas each have their own proud style of barbecue.
In the Carolinas, barbecue sauce is often vinegar-based, and in parts of South Carolina it is made with mustard.
A traditional Chicago-style hot dog is never served with ketchup.
The Philly cheesesteak was created in the 1930s by brothers Pat and Harry Olivieri.
Bacon and eggs became the classic American breakfast partly thanks to a 1920s campaign by publicist Edward Bernays for a bacon company.
Pepperoni is the most popular pizza topping in the United States.
Ranch dressing was created in the 1950s by a former plumber, Steve Henson, who served it at his dude ranch in California.
Cooking a whole hog in the traditional style can take 12 to 24 hours.

## Beer, Coffee & Whiskey
Stout and porter get their dark color and roasted flavor from malt that has been roasted like coffee beans.
Lager is brewed at colder temperatures than ale, which gives it a cleaner, crisper taste.
Hops belong to the same plant family as cannabis.
Irish coffee was invented in 1943 at an airport in Foynes, Ireland, to warm up cold passengers from transatlantic flights.
There are more barrels of bourbon aging in Kentucky than there are people living there.
Japanese whisky has been named the best whisky in the world at international competitions several times.
A single malt Scotch is made only from malted barley at a single distillery.
When coffee beans are roasted, they make a cracking sound like popcorn. Roasters call it the first crack.
Nescafé began selling instant coffee in 1938.
The first coffeehouses opened in the Middle East in the 1500s and quickly became places to talk, play chess and hear news.

## Survival & the Great Outdoors
The idea that you lose most of your body heat through your head is an exaggeration. You lose heat from any skin left uncovered.
Snow blindness is a sunburn on the surface of your eyes, caused by sunlight reflected off snow.
A thin emergency space blanket can reflect up to about 90 percent of your body heat back to you.
Most people buried by an avalanche who survive are dug out within the first 15 minutes.
Count the seconds between a lightning flash and the thunder. Every three seconds is about one kilometer of distance.
Thunder can usually be heard up to about 15 kilometers away.
Most wildfires in the United States are started by people, not by lightning.
Robert Baden-Powell, who founded the Scouts in 1907, gave them their motto: Be Prepared.
In cold water, most of the danger in the first minutes comes from the cold shock to your breathing, not from hypothermia.
Water purification tablets and boiling both make stream water safe to drink from most germs. Boiling for one minute is enough at low altitude.

## Wild Animals
A Kodiak bear, the largest kind of brown bear, can weigh up to about 680 kilograms.
A crocodile can survive for months without eating.
Wolves howl to find their pack mates and to warn other wolves away from their territory.
A hippo can open its mouth to about 150 degrees.
The king cobra is the longest venomous snake in the world, reaching up to about 5.5 meters.
The mongoose is partly resistant to snake venom, which helps it fight cobras.
Hunters nicknamed the African buffalo the black death because of how dangerous it is.
Orcas hunt great white sharks and have been seen eating just their livers.
A cheetah cannot roar. It purrs, chirps and growls instead.
A snow leopard can leap about 9 meters in a single bound.

## Movies & TV Behind the Scenes
In the original Wizard of Oz book, Dorothy's magic slippers were silver. The movie made them ruby red to show off color film.
The blood in the famous shower scene of Psycho was chocolate syrup.
The velociraptors in Jurassic Park were much bigger than the real animals, closer in size to a relative called Deinonychus.
George Lucas said the Millennium Falcon's shape was inspired by a hamburger with an olive on the side.
The falling green code in The Matrix was made from scanned characters in sushi recipe books.
The asteroid field in The Empire Strikes Back includes a potato and a shoe hidden among the rocks.
The horse's head in The Godfather was real, obtained from a dog food company.
Toy Story, released in 1995, was the first feature film made entirely with computer animation.
Clint Eastwood is said to have worn the same poncho through all three of his Dollars westerns without ever washing it.
Dan Aykroyd originally wrote Ghostbusters with his friend John Belushi in mind for one of the lead roles.

## Rock & Roll
Pete Townshend of The Who broke his first guitar by accident in 1964 when it hit a low ceiling, and then smashed it on purpose.
The Beatles' last public performance was a surprise concert on the roof of their company's office in London in 1969.
Paul McCartney woke up with the melody of Yesterday in his head and at first gave it the working title Scrambled Eggs.
Buddy Holly was only 22 years old when he died in a plane crash in 1959.
Lemmy Kilmister of Motörhead once worked as a roadie for Jimi Hendrix.
The Rolling Stones have played more than 2,000 concerts.
The first music video ever played on MTV, in 1981, was Video Killed the Radio Star.
Elvis Presley's home, Graceland, is one of the most visited private homes in America.
ZZ Top's drummer, Frank Beard, was the only member of the band without a long beard.
Guns N' Roses' Appetite for Destruction is one of the best-selling debut albums of all time.

## The Male Body
The adult male heart weighs about 300 to 350 grams, roughly the weight of a can of soda.
On average, men have more red blood cells than women.
Regularly sleeping only five hours a night can lower a young man's testosterone noticeably within a week, according to research.
A normal adult resting heart rate is 60 to 100 beats per minute. Well-trained athletes can be down near 40.
Without training, most men slowly lose muscle mass after about age 30.
The average man in the United States is about 175 centimeters tall.
Your feet have about 250,000 sweat glands between them.
The stomach can stretch to hold about 4 liters of food and drink.
The skin on your eyelids is the thinnest on your body, and the skin on the soles of your feet is the thickest.
Your heart beats around 35 million times a year.

## Odd Laws, Rules & Records
In Britain, a driver who deliberately splashes pedestrians by driving through a puddle can be fined for careless driving.
On September 3, 1967, Sweden switched from driving on the left to driving on the right overnight. It was called Dagen H.
In Singapore, you can be fined for not flushing a public toilet.
In Iceland, new baby names must be approved by an official naming committee.
In soccer, a goal cannot be scored directly from a throw-in.
In baseball, a batter can run to first base after a dropped third strike, even though he struck out.
A cricket Test match can last five full days and still end in a draw.
In boxing, a fighter who is knocked down has until the referee counts to 10 to get back up.
In Germany, drinking a beer in public is perfectly legal in most places.
In Monopoly, you can still collect rent while you are in jail.

## Gadgets & Tools
The first Swiss Army knife had four tools: a blade, a reamer, a can opener and a screwdriver.
Stone hammers are among the oldest tools ever found, more than 3 million years old.
Zip ties were invented in 1958 to bundle the wiring inside airplanes.
The Allen key, or hex key, is named after the Allen Manufacturing Company, which sold it in the early 1900s.
Gaffer tape is used on film sets and stages because it holds strong but peels off without leaving sticky residue.
The first digital watch with an LED display, the Hamilton Pulsar, went on sale in 1972 for $2,100.
The scientists who developed the lithium-ion battery won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2019.
The scroll wheel on computer mice became popular with Microsoft's IntelliMouse in 1996.
The first electric drill was invented in 1889 by Arthur James Arnot in Melbourne, Australia.
The adjustable wrench is so closely linked to one brand that many Americans just call it a crescent wrench.
